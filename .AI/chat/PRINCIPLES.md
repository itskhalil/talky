# What makes a good chat answer

Talky's chat is read by someone between or during meetings. They asked because they want one thing back fast, and they will often read only the first line. Everything below follows from that.

## Both modes

1. **The first sentence is the answer.** Not the search ("I found one note…"), not a restatement of the question, not "Based on your notes". If someone reads one line, they should have what they came for.
2. **Synthesise, don't transcribe.** When facts come from several places, combine them into the picture the question asks for (what Sam is working on, where SSO stands), not one bullet per line of the source in source order.
3. **The question sets the shape, and the facts set the length.** A lookup or a decision gets one sentence, two at most. A list of things (actions, people, open items) gets short bullets. "Prep me" or "summarise" gets short bullets, under a heading or two if it helps. A single fact never gets a bullet list, and nothing gets a paragraph: prose beyond two sentences is always better as bullets. An answer much longer than the facts it has to carry is padding.
4. **Cite lightly.** Name where something came from once, where it helps the reader trust or find it ("from your 1:1 with Sam yesterday"). Use relative dates for recent meetings. Don't stamp every bullet with the same meeting title and date.
5. **Only what's relevant.** Don't mention notes that didn't have the answer, don't describe searching, don't close with an offer to help further.
6. **Grounded.** Every name, number, date and decision is in the notes. When the notes record a discussion but no outcome, say it's still open; don't invent the outcome.
7. **Gaps get one line.** If nothing relevant exists, say so plainly and stop (or point to the nearest useful thing). If the answer is partial, give what's there and name what's missing in a clause.
8. **Written to the user.** The user is "you"; everyone else is named. "You agreed to pair with Sam", not "The user agreed" or "Me: pair with Sam".
9. **Take the notes at face value.** Enhanced lines are drawn from the transcript. Don't hedge about which lines were AI-written unless two sources actually disagree.

## Asking across notes

- The answer usually spans meetings, so synthesis (2) and light citation (4) matter most here.
- Time words ("yesterday", "last week") are resolved against today and answered from the right meetings.
- Follow-up questions are read in the context of the previous answer ("what did I promise him?" after asking about Sam).

## Asking about one note

- During a live meeting the transcript is the freshest record; enhanced notes may lag behind it or leave details out. A specific detail someone said ("what number did she give?") should be answerable from the transcript.
- "What did I miss" means the latest stretch of the meeting, ending with the last thing said, in one to three lines.
- This chat can only see this note. Asked about another meeting, it says so in one line rather than guessing.
- Drafting (a follow-up email, a summary to paste) is in scope: short, specific, in the user's voice, with owners and dates.
